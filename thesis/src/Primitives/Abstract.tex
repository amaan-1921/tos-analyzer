\abstract{\addtocontents{toc}{\vspace{1em}}
  The proliferation of digital services has led to users often
  validating Terms of Service (ToS) and privacy agreements that are
  lengthy, complicated and hard to understand in their entirety
  without legal skills. As a result, users may voluntarily agree to
  clauses that affect their privacy, financial responsibilities, and
  legal rights. In order to meet this challenge, this project
  introduces RiskWise, which introduces a web-based intelligent
  system that can automatically analyze ToS documents and also
  emphasizes potentially risky contractual clauses in an easy to
  comprehend and explainable way. The proposed architecture combines
  Natural Language Processing\index{NLP}, Knowledge Graph\index{Knowledge Graph} generation and
  Retrieval-Augmented Generation\index{RAG} in a single hybrid model.

  Uploaded documents are semantically parsed and chunked\index{Semantic Chunking} and then
  embedded into document embedding\index{Semantic Embedding} in a pattern and similarity-based
  retrieval. Concurrently, structured subject-predicate-object
  relationships\index{Triple Extraction} are taken to build a knowledge graph that reflects
  contractual entities and obligations.

  A hybrid retrieval framework\index{Hybrid Retrieval} built upon vector similarity search\index{Vector Similarity Search}
  and graph traversal\index{Graph Traversal} extracts enriched contextual information, which
  is subsequently analyzed by a generative language model\index{LLM} in order to
  detect risky clauses\index{Clause Detection}, grade them for severity\index{Risk Analysis} and generate
  user-friendly explanations\index{Explainable AI}. The functional prototype was created
  with a modular full stack model using a React\index{React} frontend, FastAPI\index{FastAPI}
  backend, and graph database\index{Graph Database} that supports relational and vector\index{Vector Database} versions.

  Case-based experimental testing validated the capability of the
  system to retrieve contextually relevant clauses, justify their
  nature, and allow interactive user exploration via conversational querying.

  These findings show that hybrid Graph-RAG architectures can indeed
  be applied to facilitate contextual completeness and
  interpretability of automated legal document analysis. RiskWise
  bridges these gaps by translating complex contractual content into
  actionable insights, helping provide transparency for the digital
  experience, supporting informed consent, and educating the audience
  of users on what they can do in a digital landscape.
}

\textbf{\textit{Keywords}:} Terms of Service Analysis, Knowledge
Graph, Retrieval-Augmented Generation, Legal NLP, Hybrid Retrieval,
Explainable AI
